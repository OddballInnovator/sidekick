# AI Infrastructure This Week
*Your five-minute briefing · October 7, 2026*

## Power is the new compute
**Black Hills Corp** will spend **$1.8 billion** (2027–2029) on 564 MW of new generation to power a proposed Google data center in Cheyenne, Wyoming. The 2.7 GW project starts deliveries late 2027, peaks 2030 — and Google covers the full cost so it doesn't hit other ratepayers. Separately, Google signed a **3.59 GW** contract with Constellation Energy ($4.3B fleet investment). The grid bottleneck is now the story, not the chips.
— [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/black-hills-plans-18-billion-investment-power-googles-data-center-2026-10-06/)

## CoreWeave's big swing: Forge
At Fully Connected (5,500 attendees, SF), **CoreWeave** launched **Forge** — a dev layer unifying Weights & Biases, OpenPipe, marimo notebooks, serverless RL, and evals into one "AI loop." Also: first production **NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72** workloads (Cognition, 4.8x inference throughput vs GB200), plus bare-metal Vera CPUs. MasterClass and Canva are already building on it.
— [Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/coreweave-fully-connected-2026-forge-brings-frontier-lab-rl-to-every-ai-team/)

## $20B for Rubin GPUs in Asia
**AM Intelligence** committed **$20 billion** and **20,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs** across India and Malaysia. Combined with Foxconn's **47% revenue jump** on AI server demand, the buildout is accelerating, not slowing.
— [AI News Brief](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-news-brief-tuesday-6-october-2026-ist-muralidhar-rao-xmnac) · [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/10/05/top-tech-news-today-october-5-2026-deutsche-telekom-foxconn-google-openai-schneider-electric-more/)

## The quiet moves
- **Schneider Electric** is making a **$22.6B** bet on industrial software and AI.
- **Tencent** is reportedly leasing 100,000 advanced chips via Oracle sites in Southeast Asia.
- **Huawei ↔ Qualcomm** signed a broad 5G/compute/AI patent cross-license.

## Bottom line
This week wasn't about models — it was about **electrons and concrete**. Power contracts, gigawatt-scale builds, and post-training platforms. If you're planning AI capacity, the constraint to watch is grid interconnection, not GPU supply.
